The Ecology of Grazing Lawns

Samuel T. Woodrich


ABSTRACT: Herbivores can instill significant changes to both local habitat characteristics and to the functions of ecosystems through herbivory and through the indirect ecological effects associated with herbivory pressure. My dissertation examines and expands upon a specific consumer-resource interaction in ecology caused by frequent and concentrated grazing by native herbivores in landscapes worldwide, deemed grazing lawns. I conducted the first systematic review of grazing lawn ecology and performed a field study that describes the characteristics, functions, and impacts of Roosevelt elk (Cervus canadensis roosevelti) and black-tailed deer (Odocoileus hemionus columbianus) maintained grazing lawns in the central Oregon Coast range. My systematic review of journal articles related to grazing lawn ecology revealed predation, and predator effects are frequently not considered in grazing lawn research, even when predators of grazing lawn maintaining herbivores are present within the study sites. I also identified various inconsistencies and deviations in the language used to describe the characteristics and functions of grazing lawns and suggest a modern definition that mitigates these discrepancies for future research on the subject. In my field study chapter, I identified and described the characteristics of grazing lawns in the temperate coastal rainforest ecosystem of the Oregon coast, a system previously not associated with grazing lawn presence. Grazing lawns in the Tenmile Creek and Cummins Creek watersheds were associated with increased cover of alien plants in the herbaceous layer, suggesting that repeated herbivory gives alien vegetation a competitive advantage over native species. Following the exclusion of herbivory in grazing lawns, alien plants were no longer present, indicating the cessation of herbivory can shift plant structure and composition towards conditions of the native forest that surround these lawns. Finally, I provide commentary on the effects frequent and intense herbivory pressure has on degraded habitats of the Tenmile Creek watershed and offer restoration prescriptions that will passively improve the ecological conditions based on the results of my grazing lawn ecology research.
